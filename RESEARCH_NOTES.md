# Research notes

## Framework demonstrated

Authority reference → preserved synthetic warning → understandable guidance → acknowledgement and action checklist → structured citizen feedback → human support-team review.

The contribution being illustrated is communication and anticipatory action support. The prototype is not a novel hazard forecasting model. Rainfall visualization adds spatial context; it does not calculate flood or lightning risk.

## Scenario contrast

| Dimension | Flood — Kurigram | Lightning — Kurigram |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Synthetic lead time | 24–48 hours | Within 30 minutes |
| Communication focus | Preparation and household support | Rapid understanding and shelter |
| Action pattern | Essentials, vulnerable household members, official updates | Stop outdoor activity, safe shelter, remain sheltered after thunder |
| Authority reference | FFWC / BWDB | BMD |

## Evaluation conditions

A: conventional warning only. B: the same warning plus actionable guidance. Manual condition selection and local response capture allow instrument demonstration. They do not provide controlled assignment, blinding, recruitment, participant management or analysis of a completed study.

Measurements include comprehension, protective-action choice, perceived relevance, risk perception, preparedness intention, trust, usability and time from condition selection to submission. A future study should distinguish intention from observed behavior and analyze hazards separately.

## Ethics and deployment boundaries

Use fictional details only. No personal data is needed. Requests reach only the local browser dashboard, not emergency services. No automatic evacuation order, independent prediction, verified rescue response or real-world readiness determination is made. Field deployment requires expert validation, ethics approval, consent design, accessibility review, security work and operational partnerships.
